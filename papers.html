Selected working papers

Four views of the current EOS research program. Each paper states the boundary of its evidence.

Architecture hypothesis

Evidence-gated causal world models

A proposed split between a high-capacity predictor and an epistemic governor that controls persistent change. This is a research architecture, not evidence that general intelligence has been achieved.

Governance study

Dual governance for continual agents

Why buying information and changing a model permanently should not inherit authority from the same evidence. The experiments are narrow supplied-mechanism studies.

Component study

Typed predictive-law operators

A controlled study of learning reusable mechanism operators, composing them into unseen joint laws, and using residual channels to decide what kind of investigation is needed.

World-model methods

Consequence-certified state refinement

A formal account of when a partially observed model may merge histories, refine its predictive state, or admit new structure, together with bounded component evidence and explicit non-claims.

Earlier research record

Five additional papers from the broader Sonne research program. They use the same archive passphrase and are decrypted only in your browser.

Research paper

When do spiking-convolutional hybrids help?

Some neural networks work in bursts. This paper asks when that helps under a fair comparison, and records the cases where the apparent advantage disappears.

Research paper

What can an illumination guarantee cover?

A careful study of brightness changes that preserve the ordering of image values, with ties and ordinary pixel-space limits kept visible.

Research program

Project SOL: when should a system change?

The main EOS line: a system predicts first, tests whether the surprise is real, and keeps a possible new mechanism separate until the evidence is checked.

Working paper

Confirmation-gated structural growth

A closer look at how SOL-IPS records the exact moment a candidate crosses its evidence threshold and links that moment back to the learner.

Evidence note

Counterexamples, not certificates

A reminder that finding one case where a structural decision fails is valuable evidence, not a reason to claim a broad guarantee.
