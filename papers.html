Current EOS papers

Four papers describe the current EOS research program. Each one separates the proposal, experiment, and limit.

Architecture hypothesis

Evidence-gated causal world models

Proposes separating the part of a system that predicts from the part that decides whether a permanent change should be allowed. This is an architecture proposal, not evidence of general intelligence.

Governance study

Dual governance for continual agents

Tests why gathering more information and permanently changing a model should require different evidence. The experiments cover only the supplied mechanisms.

Component study

Typed predictive-law operators

Tests whether a system can learn reusable rules, combine them in new ways, and use its errors to choose the next investigation.

World-model methods

Consequence-certified state refinement

Defines when a model with incomplete information may treat two histories as equivalent, make its internal state more detailed, or add new structure. The evidence covers individual components, not a complete system.

Earlier papers

Five earlier papers from the broader research program. They use the same passphrase and are unlocked only on your device.

Research paper

When do spiking-convolutional hybrids help?

Compares a spiking hybrid with a matched conventional network and records where the estimated advantage appears and where it disappears.

Research paper

What can an illumination guarantee cover?

Studies brightness changes that preserve the order of image values and states the unresolved limits around tied values and ordinary pixel space.

Research program

Project SOL: when should a system change?

Introduces the main EOS question: should a learning system reuse, repair, wait, or add new structure when new information arrives?

Working paper

Confirmation-gated structural growth

Examines how SOL-IPS records the moment a possible new structure receives enough evidence to move forward.

Evidence note

Counterexamples, not certificates

Shows why one failing example is useful evidence against a broad structural guarantee, but is not a proof of general failure.
