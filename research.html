Claims should earn their confidence.

We ask a simple question: what survives a fair test? That means matched comparisons, fresh data, saved failures, and plain limits on every result.

Spiking intelligence

When do spiking-convolutional hybrids help?

Some neural networks work with small bursts of activity. That is interesting, but it does not automatically make them better.

Our controlled study tests a content-adaptive spiking neuron called CA-LIF. Several apparent wins disappeared when we compared like with like. One narrower result remained: in a Fashion-MNIST comparison at low timestep counts, the hybrid used fewer estimated operations than the matched dense network.

What this does not show

The advantage disappeared on CIFAR-10, a colour-image benchmark, because preparing the input dominated the estimate. We did not measure energy on real hardware, so this is not a general efficiency claim.

Continual learning

When should a model reuse, repair, or grow?

A system that keeps learning needs a way to decide whether a surprise is truly new.

Project SOL is the earlier research program; EOS is the current system built from it. The controller can ask whether to reuse, combine, refine, recalibrate, repair, wait, or investigate a genuinely new mechanism. It must predict before learning from each confirmation observation, and a candidate stays quarantined until the exact evidence-earning state is recorded.

What this does not show

The results support a carefully scoped protocol for controlled adaptation. They do not prove a generally intelligent agent, unlimited lifelong growth, or a measured neuromorphic energy advantage.

EOS-F1

Can the research system fail safely while it is still learning?

EOS-F1 is an ongoing small-model and systems track, not a finished general-purpose model.

The grounded interface can retrieve hash-bound evidence, refuse unsupported claims, abstain when its catalogue has no answer, replay a typed simulation, and withhold generation when a model has not earned release. A 0.6B pilot trained and reloaded correctly, but passed only 7 of 12 fixed boundary probes, so generation remained withheld.

A separate dense continued-pretraining baseline improved held-out next-token loss and retained two small task subsets. A matched sparse-specialist design then lost to the dense control, and even an oracle could not rescue those experts. That specialist lane is closed rather than being presented as a win.

What this does not show

EOS-F1 is not ready for a public model release. It does not establish broad reasoning, 3D cognition, hardware efficiency, autonomous research, or general intelligence.

Robustness

What can an illumination guarantee actually cover?

A guarantee is only as broad as the change it was written to cover.

Our construction compares the ordering of image values, then studies what happens when brightness changes without changing that ordering. In that setting, the model can be exactly unchanged.

What this does not show

The result covers the declared class of brightness changes in rank space. Ties, flat regions, and a guarantee about ordinary pixel values remain open limits.
