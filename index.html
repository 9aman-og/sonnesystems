Research

Four questions we are actively testing.

Each question has a defined comparison, a measurable result, and a clear limit.

Can event-based networks do less work?

We compare spiking and conventional networks under matched conditions to find where event-based processing helps and where it adds cost.

When should a model reuse, repair, or grow?

When new information arrives, we test whether the model should reuse existing knowledge, repair it, wait, or add new structure.

What can a model safely ignore?

We study changes, such as brightness shifts, that should not alter a result, then state exactly where the guarantee stops.

When should a system refuse to answer or ship?

We test whether a system can show its evidence, refuse unsupported answers, and remain unavailable when fixed checks fail.

Results so far

Clear findings, including the failures.

These are limited results from specific experiments, not broad claims about AI.

Research paper

A spiking hybrid helped on one dataset, not another.

It used fewer estimated operations on Fashion-MNIST at low timestep counts. The advantage disappeared on CIFAR-10.
